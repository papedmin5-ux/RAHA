Explore Your Menstrual Cycle

Learn about the four phases of your cycle.

01. Menstrual Phase

Understand what happens during your period.

The menstrual phase marks the beginning of the menstrual cycle. During this phase, the uterus sheds its previously built-up lining because pregnancy has not occurred. This process causes menstrual bleeding, which usually lasts between 2 and 7 days. Some people may experience cramps, tiredness, headaches, or mood changes. Getting enough rest, staying hydrated, and using suitable menstrual products can help improve comfort during this time.

02. Follicular Phase

Discover how your body prepares for ovulation.

The follicular phase begins on the first day of menstruation and continues until ovulation. During this phase, the brain releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), which encourages small follicles in the ovaries to develop. Usually, one follicle becomes dominant and contains an egg that continues to mature. Estrogen levels also increase, helping the uterus rebuild its lining in preparation for a possible pregnancy.

03. Ovulation Phase

Learn about the release of an egg.

Ovulation is the stage when a mature egg is released from the ovary. It usually happens around the middle of the menstrual cycle, although the exact timing varies from person to person and from cycle to cycle. This process is triggered by a rise in luteinizing hormone (LH). The egg travels into the fallopian tube, where it may meet sperm. This is generally the most fertile period of the cycle.

04. Luteal Phase

Understand the changes that occur after ovulation.

The luteal phase begins after ovulation and continues until the next menstrual period. During this phase, the empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which produces progesterone. This hormone helps maintain the uterine lining in case pregnancy occurs. If fertilization does not happen, hormone levels decrease and the uterine lining eventually sheds, starting a new menstrual cycle. Some people may experience PMS symptoms such as bloating, breast tenderness, mood changes, or fatigue.
